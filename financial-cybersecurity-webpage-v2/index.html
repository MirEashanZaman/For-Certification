Tips for Financial Cybersecurity

Protecting your financial accounts online starts with consistent everyday habits. Follow these seven practical tips to keep your money and personal information safe from cyber threats.

1. Create Strong, Unique Passwords

Use a unique, complex password for every banking, payment, and investment account. Mix uppercase and lowercase letters, numbers, and symbols, and avoid personal details like birthdays. A trusted password manager can generate and store strong credentials safely so you never reuse passwords across websites.

2. Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

Turn on multi-factor authentication wherever available. MFA adds an essential second layer of security by requiring a one-time code, authenticator app approval, or biometric check alongside your password. Even if someone steals your login details, they cannot access your account without that extra verification.

3. Watch Out for Phishing Scams

Be cautious of unexpected emails, texts, or phone calls claiming urgent issues with your account or asking for personal information. Never click suspicious links or open unknown attachments. Instead, visit your bank's official website directly or call the number printed on your card.

4. Monitor Accounts and Set Up Alerts

Check your bank and credit card statements regularly to catch unauthorized transactions early. Enable real-time email or text alerts for withdrawals, large purchases, and login attempts. Reporting unfamiliar activity quickly helps limit potential financial loss.

5. Avoid Banking on Public Wi-Fi

Public Wi-Fi networks in cafes, airports, and hotels are often unsecured, making it easier for attackers to intercept sensitive data. Avoid accessing banking apps or transferring money on public networks. Use a private home network, mobile data, or a trusted VPN instead.

6. Keep Devices and Software Updated

Install operating system, browser, and mobile banking app updates promptly. Updates deliver critical security patches that fix known vulnerabilities. Combine regular updates with reputable antivirus software and automatic screen locks to protect your accounts if a device is lost or stolen.

7. Limit Shared Information and Freeze Your Credit

Be mindful of personal details you share online, since information like your address or birth date can help criminals bypass security questions. Consider placing a free credit freeze with major credit bureaus to stop identity thieves from opening new accounts in your name.

Stay vigilant and review your account security settings regularly.
